# CIFAR-10N: learning from messy human labels

Shibam Sundar Mahakud (24B2232). Figures are in `analysis/`. The commands to reproduce each task are in `code/README.md`.

**Q1.** Visually similar classes dominate. Counting both directions of each unordered pair: automobile↔truck accounts for 2,373 wrong labels (13.1% of all 18,109), cat↔dog 1,758 (9.7%), deer↔horse 916 (5.1%) and airplane↔ship 748 (4.1%). Deer↔dog, bird↔frog and airplane↔bird follow. The top four pairs cover 32% of the noise and the top ten cover 52%. By true class, cat and deer are mislabeled most often (51% each) and horse least often (28%). The overall noise rate is 40.24%. The noise is class-dependent and concentrated on look-alike vehicles and animals, unlike uniform noise (`task1_confusion.png`).

**Q2.** Memorization starts around epoch 37–40. Through epoch 30, the network predicts the wrong noisy label on only 11–16% of mislabeled images and their true class on up to 66%. Clean validation accuracy peaks at 0.784 (epoch 37) and plateaus until epoch 43. Meanwhile the memorized fraction bends upward, from 0.19 at epoch 40 to 0.69 at epoch 60. True-class predictions fall to 20% and clean validation accuracy to 0.675. The bend matches the cosine schedule's tail: memorization accelerates once the learning rate drops below about 0.025. Early stopping by noisy validation accuracy picks epoch 36 (0.549) (`task2_dynamics.png`).

**Q3.** AUM was the best single signal (AUROC 0.960, precision@k 0.874), ahead of the cross-validated margin (0.953) and the cross-validated 1−p(label) (0.946). The submitted rank average reached AUROC 0.963 with the same precision@k. Failure mode: systematic confusions. At k, the held-out model agrees with the wrong label on 77% of the 2,292 missed errors, mostly cats labeled dog, trucks labeled automobile and deer labeled horse. Humans and networks confuse the same ambiguous images, so these errors look clean. In the other direction, hard but correctly labeled images get flagged, such as birds against sky and tiny cars (`task3_failures.png`).

**Q4.** Filter-and-retrain was best: 0.573 noisy validation accuracy, against 0.549 for early-stopped cross-entropy. GCE scored 0.566 and co-teaching 0.564. Filtering helped because the kept set was nearly clean. Dropping the top 52.5% by score removed 96% of the wrong labels and left 21,362 images with 3.2% noise. The cost was discarding 23% of the correct images, because the label-free noise estimate overshot. Caveat: rerunning the identical pipeline moved early-stopped cross-entropy by 1.1 points (0.560 to 0.549), so the gaps between methods are within run-to-run noise. Only "every method beats cross-entropy" held in both runs.

**Q5.** No. Filter-and-retrain beat GCE, which beat cross-entropy, under both noise types. But the gains over early-stopped cross-entropy grew on synthetic noise: +3.0 and +2.7 points, against +2.4 and +1.7 on real noise. GCE nearly caught up. Random flips contradict clear visual evidence, so they are memorized later and less: 5% at epoch 40 and 43% at epoch 60, against 19% and 69% for real noise. Margins split into two clean groups (AUM AUROC 0.989 vs 0.959), and the label-free estimate became accurate (39.1% vs true 40.1%). Human errors sit on ambiguous images and look like hard clean examples.
